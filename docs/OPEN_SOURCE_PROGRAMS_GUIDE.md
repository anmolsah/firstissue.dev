# The Ultimate Guide to Open Source Programs & Fellowships

Comprehensive guide covering major global open source programs, mentorships, fellowships, and how developers can prepare, apply, and succeed.

---

## 1. Google Summer of Code (GSoC)

### What is GSoC?
Google Summer of Code (GSoC) is a global, online program focused on bringing new contributors into open source software development. GSoC contributors work with an open source organization on a 12+ week programming project under the guidance of mentors.

### Eligibility & Timeline
- **Eligibility**: Open to students and beginner open source contributors (18+ years old).
- **Timeline**:
  - **January - February**: Open source organizations apply to participate.
  - **Late February**: Mentoring organizations announced by Google.
  - **March - April**: Contributor proposal submission period.
  - **May**: Accepted contributor projects announced. Community Bonding period begins.
  - **June - August**: Coding period (Midterm evaluation around week 6, Final evaluation around week 12). Extended projects can run up to 22 weeks.
  - **September - November**: Results announced and certificates issued.

### How to Prepare (The Winning Strategy)
1. **Start 2-3 Months Early (January/February)**:
   - Check previous years' organizations at [summerofcode.withgoogle.com](https://summerofcode.withgoogle.com). Many orgs return year after year (e.g., Apache, Linux Foundation, Python Software Foundation, Mozilla, KDE, CNCF).
   - Filter by programming language and technology stack you know or want to learn.
2. **Join the Community Channels**:
   - Join their Slack, Discord, Zulip, Matrix, or IRC channels.
   - Introduce yourself warmly with your background, timezone, and your interest in contributing to their GSoC ideas list.
3. **Make Early Pre-Proposal Contributions**:
   - Organizations prioritize candidates who have already landed PRs.
   - Solve 2-4 beginner issues ("good first issue", documentation, bug fixes) before submitting your proposal.
4. **Drafting a Winning GSoC Proposal**:
   - **Executive Summary**: Brief problem statement and your solution.
   - **Technical Architecture**: Detailed explanation of modules, APIs, database changes, and design patterns.
   - **Weekly Timeline**: Detailed week-by-week milestones with deliverables, testing plans, and buffer time.
   - **About Me & Past Contributions**: Links to your merged PRs, GitHub profile, and portfolio.
   - **Commitment & Time Management**: State your availability (e.g., 30 hours/week) and mention any exams or vacations upfront.
   - **Post-GSoC Roadmap**: State your commitment to continue maintaining the feature after GSoC concludes.

## 2. Linux Foundation Mentorship (LFX)

### What is LFX Mentorship?
The Linux Foundation Mentorship (LFX) connects aspiring developers with top open source projects across the Linux ecosystem, including Kubernetes, Cloud Native Computing Foundation (CNCF), Hyperledger, GraphQL, and OpenSSF.

### Key Details:
- Runs in **Spring**, **Summer**, and **Fall** cohorts (3 times per year).
- Full-time and part-time options available.
- Offers an international stipend based on location.
- High industry prestige: Direct mentorship from senior maintainers and core engineers.

### How to Apply:
1. Create a profile on the [LFX Mentorship Portal](https://mentorship.lfx.linuxfoundation.org/).
2. Upload your resume and link your GitHub profile.
3. Submit a statement of purpose and cover letter explaining your familiarity with the project's domain (e.g., Go, Rust, distributed systems, kernel, or security).
4. Actively engage in the project's GitHub issues and mailing lists before applying.

## 3. Hacktoberfest

### Overview
Hacktoberfest is an annual month-long event (every October) organized by DigitalOcean, Cloudflare, and partners to celebrate open source software.

### Official Rules & Participation:
- Register on the official Hacktoberfest website during October.
- Submit **4 valid pull requests** to any public repository with the `hacktoberfest` topic or label between October 1 and October 31.
- PRs must be merged by the maintainer or labeled `hacktoberfest-accepted`.
- **Quality Matters**: PRs that only change whitespace, fix single-character typos in READMEs, or spam repositories will be marked as `invalid` or `spam`, which disqualifies the contributor from the entire event.

### What Counts as a High-Quality PR?
- Fixing genuine reported bugs.
- Writing unit and integration tests.
- Translating documentation or adding detailed technical examples.
- Refactoring legacy code or migrating to modern frameworks.

## 4. Outreachy

### Overview
Outreachy provides 3-month paid internships for individuals subject to systemic bias or underrepresented in tech. Internships run twice a year (May to August, and December to March).

### Key Features:
- **100% Remote**: Work from anywhere in the world.
- **$7,000 USD Total Stipend** + $500 travel/career stipend.
- Projects include coding, documentation, UX design, user research, and data science across Wikimedia, Mozilla, GNOME, Fedora, and Debian.

### Selection Process:
1. **Initial Application**: Essays on systemic bias, discrimination, and time commitment.
2. **Contribution Phase**: Accepted applicants contribute actively to participating projects for one month.
3. **Final Selection**: Mentors select interns based on the quality and collaboration of their contribution-phase PRs.

## 5. Major Fellowships: MLH & Summer of Bitcoin

### Major League Hacking (MLH) Fellowship
- 12-week educational internship program where fellows contribute to open source projects used by millions of developers.
- Tracks: Software Engineering, Site Reliability Engineering (SRE), and Web3.
- Includes pair programming, daily standups, code reviews, and mentorship from full-time engineers.

### Summer of Bitcoin
- Global mentorship program introducing university students to Bitcoin open source development and design.
- Intensive focus on C++, Rust, cryptography, network protocols, and privacy.
- Offers competitive stipends and post-program employment opportunities with Bitcoin startups and core teams.
